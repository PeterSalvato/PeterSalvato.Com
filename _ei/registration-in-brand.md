Registration is a screen printing term. Each color in a multi-color print is a separate plate. You build them independently, each carrying its own ink. The final image only exists when the plates combine. If one plate shifts, the image misregisters. The colors don't line up. You see the gap between what was intended and what printed.

[Aiden Jae](/output/aiden-jae/) is a registration problem across four layers: photography, identity, platform, and packaging. Each layer was built independently. Each one carries its own information. The brand only exists when the layers register.

---

The photography layer proves the product. Lighting that shows how gold actually catches light. No retouching that hides the hand-finishing. Variable aspect ratios because each piece has different visual weight. The system doesn't force the photograph into a preset box. The photography carries the brand directly. When you see the actual texture of recycled gold under honest light, you're seeing the quality and the sourcing story without reading a word.

The identity layer carries the register. Clean typographic wordmark. A palette pulled from the materials: warm browns, muted sage, cream. Typography that stays out of the way. The visual language feels restrained without performing luxury, because the product is the luxury, not the branding. These are rules, not preferences. Every visual element either reinforces what the product actually is or it gets cut.

The platform layer serves the other two. Custom Liquid templates, SCSS framework, product page architecture designed so the typography, spacing, and hierarchy reinforce the identity the photography establishes. A generic Shopify template would override both. The platform serves the brand instead of replacing it.

The packaging layer closes the loop. Wool felt pouches, sewn in-house. When the package arrives, the material the customer touches tells the same story the photography started.

---

Registration means each layer can be built, tested, and maintained independently. I can adjust the photography direction without rebuilding the platform. I can update the Shopify templates without touching the identity system. The layers are decoupled in construction but coupled in experience. Change one and the others don't break. Misalign one and the customer feels it.

At [Altrueism](/output/altrueism/), the registration architecture runs the same way. Hand-drawn marks (proving the human hand), restrained typography (proving discipline), earthen palette (proving materiality). Each layer independent, each one load-bearing. Remove the marks and the brand claims transparency without proving it. Remove the restraint and the brand looks like every other craft brand. The layers have to register.

That's what makes registration different from coordination. Coordination means the departments talked to each other. Registration means the layers were designed to produce one coherent experience when they combine, and any misalignment between them is visible. Pull any piece out and the rest doesn't hold.
