---

I started making electronic music in the early 2000s. Dirty analog synths, magnetic tape, feedback loops. No audience, no commercial goal. Just a room full of gear and whatever came out of it. The music had a texture: warm, degraded, physical. Tape hiss and overdrive and the specific sound of a signal pushed too hard through old hardware.

The problem was the packaging. Every time I made something I wanted to release, the cover art was a separate project. Finish the music, then go find a visual treatment that "fits." The cover and the music coexisted without cohering. Two processes, two aesthetics, one label slapped across both.

---

Most album art is made after the music is done. The designer listens to the tracks, interprets a mood, produces a visual that references the feeling. The result is illustration: a picture of what the music sounds like to someone else. The visual and sonic layers are related by interpretation, not by structure.

I wanted to know what it looks like when both layers come from the same system. When the visual treatment isn't referencing the sound but is built from the same material logic.

---

**Direct correlation, not illustration.** Every visual element maps to a sonic property through the same physical process:

- Tape hiss becomes halftone dot patterns. Visual grain that behaves like audio grain: present everywhere, varying in density, part of the texture rather than an effect applied on top.
- Audio distortion becomes print misregistration. The visual artifact of misalignment (plates shifted a pixel off center) mirrors the sonic artifact of overdrive (signal pushed past the threshold of clean reproduction).
- Magnetic tape becomes MICR typography. The typeface from the bottom of bank checks: a bureaucratic encoding system applied to personal experimental music. Institutional form containing obsessive content.

**Releases as archival documents.** Series codes, catalog numbers, album art treated as field manuals. The packaging presents itself as institutional documentation. That tension (institutional form, personal obsession) is the identity of the project.

**Both surfaces are mine.** [Aiden Jae](/evidence/aiden-jae/) integrates photography direction with platform architecture, but the visual and brand work are mine while the product is Randi's. MathOnTape is the version where I control both sides: the music and the visual system are built by the same person, from the same material logic, at the same time.

---

The visual treatment runs through [PressWorks](/practice/pressworks/): the same halftone screening and misregistration that produces the site's texture. The correlation works because both layers describe degradation, signal pushed through physical media until the artifacts become the aesthetic. The brand system and the music are the same argument made in two materials.
