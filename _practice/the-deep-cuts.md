I grew up in New York in the 80s and 90s. Hip-hop was in the air before you had opinions about it. Club and rave culture came later: Tunnel, Twilo, nights that started after midnight and ended whenever they ended. DJing was never a career consideration. It was how you loved music.

I've been DJing since then. Not for crowds, not for a dance floor. Ambient tracks, sound effects, sound design, music that sets a temperature rather than a beat. Almost nothing exists for people who DJ the way I do, as aesthetic exploration, as a way of hearing. Almost all DJ content is written for people who want to perform for other people.

---

*The Deep Cuts* is the attempt to write that guide. Its proposal: make the listener the architect. The moment you're responsible for what plays next, you have to hear relationships, recognize connections, understand how meaning assembles when order changes. DJing is not the goal. It is the forcing function.

Four dimensions structure the curriculum. **Selection:** hear what distinguishes one piece from another. **Transition:** recognize structural similarities that allow connection. **Sequence:** understand how meaning changes when order changes. **Timing:** grasp rhythm's multiple scales operating simultaneously.

The book is being built as a crafted physical object: illustrated spreads, diagrams, visual rhythm that mirrors the sonic content. The voice is a music nerd as mentor. The design is part of the argument: if structure in music is worth understanding, the document that teaches it should demonstrate structural thinking in its own form.

The method works. What I'm still figuring out: how to teach a way of hearing to people who've spent a lifetime listening passively.
