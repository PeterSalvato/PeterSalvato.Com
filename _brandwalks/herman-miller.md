opener: |
  Same eight lenses. This one is personal. The Eames methodology is my methodology: across materials, the same operation everywhere, the furniture and the films and the toys all one practice. "The best for the most for the least." I've studied that line the way other people study scripture. So when I read Herman Miller's web presence, I'm reading the company that inherited that practice and asking whether they held it or filed it under "lounge seating."

  What I found is a site that holds individual pieces with care and loses the whole. The Eames designer page is the best thing on the site. The homepage is the worst. Everything in between is competent commerce with no one attuned to the system.
analysis: |
  The front door is a routing page. "What would you like to do?" Two boxes: "Shop for Home" and "Plan for Workplaces." One sends you to store.hermanmiller.com. The other keeps you on hermanmiller.com. A company that holds the Eames legacy, the Nelson legacy, Noguchi, Girard, opens its website with a traffic cop. No position. No atmosphere. No room built. Just a fork in the road. The first thing Herman Miller says to a visitor is "which department are you looking for?"

  I think about what that routing page means [structurally](/practice/input-first-design/). It means there are two sites. The catalog site (hermanmiller.com) presents products with designer attribution, specs, heritage narratives. The store site (store.hermanmiller.com) presents products with pricing, configuration swatches, and a 3D configurator. The Eames Lounge Chair exists in both places. On the catalog side, the Eames grandchildren provide commentary about the design's legacy. On the store side, it's $7,925 with a 3.6-star rating and a "Buy Now" button. Same chair, two completely different relationships with the visitor. Nobody is reading those two presentations as one system. The [coherence](/practice/lens-extraction/) breaks at the seam between catalog and commerce.

  The Eames designer page is where the [fidelity](/practice/voice-governance/) lives. Charles and Ray presented as a collaborative partnership. The three guiding questions preserved: Does it interest us? Can we make it better? Will we have "serious fun" doing it? The cross-disciplinary range documented: plywood to fiberglass to film to toys, "one thing always seemed to lead to another." The 30-year-flash quote. The Vitra distribution split explained. Thirty-nine products linked. This page understands the practice. It knows that the furniture and the films and the toys were one operation. But this page sits three clicks deep in the site architecture. Nobody arrives here by accident.

  The gaming section is the sharpest break. "OWN YOUR GAME." "UNLOCK YOUR POWER SEAT." "THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A WIN AND A WINNER." Performance language, competitive framing, Logitech G partnership branding. Zero Eames presence. Zero design heritage. Zero connection to the company that put a molded plywood chair in MoMA. The gaming pages read like a different company wrote them, because a different company effectively did. This is the MillerKnoll acquisition logic visible in the site architecture: Herman Miller as a brand name applied to market segments rather than a design position held across contexts.

  The Eames Molded Plastic Chair page does something right. It names the Cranbrook origin. It traces the material evolution from plywood to fiberglass to recycled plastic. It includes the line that matters: "the best for the most for the least." It quotes Charles on the designer as host. The [scaffold](/practice/accommodation-design/) of the Eames philosophy is present on this one product page. But then you look at the related products and the chair sits next to the Zeph and the Setu and the Sayl with no hierarchy distinguishing a seventy-five-year design legacy from last year's task chair. The page builds a room and then the product grid flattens it.

  I keep returning to what the Eames practice actually was. Charles and Ray worked across furniture, film, exhibition design, graphic design, toys. Every medium was the same investigation: what does this material want to do, and how do we make the best version of it available to the most people? That practice, that [attunement](/practice/lens-extraction/) to material and audience simultaneously, is the most valuable thing Herman Miller inherited. The site treats it as product history. Background for a purchase decision. The films aren't on the site. The exhibitions aren't documented. The toys appear as accessories. The practice has been disassembled into product categories.

  The "Part of the MillerKnoll collective" tagline in the footer tells you the structural truth. Herman Miller is a brand inside a holding company. The site architecture reflects that. Each product category operates independently. The designer pages provide connective tissue but they're deep in the hierarchy, behind the routing page and the product grids and the ergonomic quiz. A visitor who enters through the gaming section and a visitor who enters through the Eames Lounge Chair page arrive in two different companies. Nobody is holding the whole the way D.J. De Pree held it when he hired George Nelson and Nelson brought in the Eameses. That was [attunement](/practice/voice-governance/) at the organizational level. The site has no equivalent. The pieces hold. The joints fail. Nobody is reading the system on the other end.
