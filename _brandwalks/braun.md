opener: |
  Same eight lenses. Braun is personal for me. I named one of my evaluation lenses "Economy" after Rams. The T3 pocket radio, the SK 4 record player, the ET 66 calculator. These objects taught a generation of designers that restraint is a position, not a compromise. Apple's entire design language through the Ive era was a direct reading of Rams. So I wanted to see what happens when you point the lenses at the web presence of the company that made those objects.

  What I found is two companies sharing one URL. A grooming products retailer running a P&G e-commerce operation, and a design heritage museum accessible only through the UK site's basement. The split is total. The US site has no design page at all. The flagship product page describes "899 unique holes" in a shaver foil. The Dieter Rams biography lives three clicks deep on a regional variant. One hundred years of the most influential industrial design program in history, and the front door is a razor promotion.
analysis: |
  The US homepage is a consumer electronics storefront. IPL devices, electric shavers, epilators, a newsletter signup offering $125 off. The navigation reads: Products, Special Offers, Tips & Tutorials, BraunCare+, About Braun, Service. Six links. Four are transactional. "About Braun" on the US site returns a 404. The page does not exist.

  The UK site tells a different story. The same "About Braun" navigation slot links to a page called "Design & Craftsmanship" with the tagline "Built to last, every day." From there you can reach a designers page that traces the lineage: Artur and Erwin Braun collaborating with the Ulm School of Design in the 1950s. Hans Gugelot and Otl Aicher on the early radio lines. Rams arriving in 1961 and leading design for 34 years. Peter Schneider redesigning the logo. Oliver Grabes running design since 2009 under the philosophy "the Strength of Pure." There's a Virgil Abloh collaboration page. A Good Design Masterclass with Ilse Crawford. A "100 years of Good Design" heritage section. The bones are there.

  But the bones are regional. A visitor arriving at braun.com from the United States gets redirected to us.braun.com, where none of this exists. The design heritage, the Rams principles, the designer biographies, the centennial celebration: all of it lives behind a geographic gate. The company's most valuable [structural scaffold](/practice/input-first-design/) is invisible to its largest market.

  The product pages are competent P&G e-commerce. The Series 9 PRO+ lists five synchronized shaving elements, a ProLift blade with titanium coating, a Pro SensoAdapt that analyzes beard density 300 times per second. The copy is benefit-driven, comparison-heavy, specification-forward. "31% higher rating on efficiency." "899 unique holes." "12% higher density of blades." This is how you sell razors. It is not how Braun used to talk about anything.

  Rams wrote that good design is as little design as possible. The product pages are as much copy as possible. Feature cards, comparison charts, SmartCare Center breakdowns, FAQ sections answering sixteen questions about electric shavers. The density is the opposite of the principle. I think the gap between "as little design as possible" and "899 unique holes" is the entire reading in one measurement.

  The Skin i-expert IPL page pushes further from the heritage. Gradient color schemes (gold to purple to blue), app integration workflows, "The World's 1st Smart IPL" positioning. This page could belong to any direct-to-consumer beauty tech brand launched in 2024. There is nothing on it that says Braun except the logo in the header. The [fidelity](/practice/voice-governance/) to the design identity is zero.

  What happened here is legible. P&G acquired Braun's consumer divisions. The products moved into a portfolio managed by conversion metrics and category benchmarks. The design heritage became a brand asset maintained by a different team in a different region with different priorities. Nobody killed the Rams legacy. They just stopped connecting it to the thing people see when they visit the site. The scaffold is intact. The joints between the scaffold and the storefront are gone. Nobody is [attuned](/practice/practitioner-in-the-loop/) to the whole because the whole doesn't exist in any single org chart.

  The 404 situation is telling. The US "About Braun" link goes nowhere. The UK design pages exist but most sub-URLs return 404s. The Good Design Masterclass, the Virgil Abloh collaboration, the centennial pages: all referenced from the design-and-craftsmanship hub, most of them dead links. The heritage section is a lobby with locked doors. Someone built the directory. Nobody maintained the rooms.

  The BraunCare+ warranty program and the service architecture are well-built. Clean category routing, product-type selection, type-number search. The support infrastructure works the way you'd expect from P&G: thorough, functional, friction-minimized. This is the part of the site where operational competence shows. The irony is that Rams would have approved of this section. It solves a problem clearly. It stays out of the way. The support pages are more Braun than the product pages.

  The AI question is uncomfortable. The product copy on this site reads like it was already generated by a content pipeline optimized for conversion. "Braun's best. Better than ever." "Ultimate closeness on 1, 3, or 7 day beards." "100% control of your style." This is template language at scale. AI tools would accelerate the production of this copy without changing its character at all. The design heritage pages, the ones that require someone to understand why Rams matters and how that connects to a shaver sold today, those are the pages that need a practitioner. And those are the pages that are 404ing.
