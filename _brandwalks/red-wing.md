opener: |
  Same eight lenses. My father was a general superintendent on construction sites. Red Wing boots were on those sites every day. So this one hits a specific frequency for me. The brand represents a world I grew up next to, a world where quality meant something because the work was dangerous and the tools had to hold.

  I wanted to see if the website holds the way the boots do.
analysis: |
  Red Wing has two buildings on one foundation. The Work side sells safety-toe boots with ASTM ratings and puncture-resistant midsoles. The Heritage side sells the Iron Ranger and the Classic Moc to people who want the construction lineage without the construction site. Both are real. Both are legitimate. And the website treats them as two separate stores that share a checkout.

  The raw materials for [coherence](/practice/lens-extraction/) are all here. A tannery in Minnesota operating since 1872. Three American factories. A repair shop that will resole your boots. A Wall of Honor program that puts actual tradespeople's worn boots on display. A "Will Your Wings" program that turns boots into heirlooms with personalized tags. These are not marketing gestures. These are structural commitments to the relationship between maker, product, and wearer. The [scaffold](/practice/input-first-design/) for a unified brand story is already built. Nobody is using it.

  The Iron Ranger product page mentions that the boot was originally built for iron miners in the 1930s. That detail appears once, in a paragraph, surrounded by specs. It doesn't connect to the Wall of Honor, which celebrates working people. It doesn't connect to the tannery page, which explains where the leather comes from. It doesn't connect to the Made in USA page, which names the three factories. Every piece of the story exists. No piece references any other piece. The [fidelity](/practice/voice-governance/) of each individual page is high. The attunement across pages is zero.

  I think about what happens when AI tools enter this content pipeline. Red Wing's product catalog is clean and well-organized, exactly the kind of content that scales easily with generation tools. The heritage stories are specific and human, exactly the kind of content that doesn't. Without someone [attuned to the whole](/practice/practitioner-in-the-loop/), the catalog grows while the heritage stays buried in the Stories section. The split widens. The Work customer never sees the heritage. The Heritage customer never sees the work. And the thing that makes Red Wing actually different from every other boot company, that the same hands in the same factory build both, disappears into two separate navigation trees.
